# Domain: product safety, sectoral legislation, imports, environment

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section C. All verified live.

## When it applies

When physical goods are sold to consumers (and, for some rules, to professionals), including via dropshipping or marketplaces.

## Step 1: determine the role in the supply chain

For each product or category:
- **manufacturer**: makes the product or markets it under its own name or brand;
- **importer**: first places a product from outside the EU on the EU market;
- **distributor**: makes an already placed product available on the market;
- **fulfilment service provider**;
- **online marketplace provider**.

Caution: own branding or substantial modification can turn a distributor into a manufacturer. Verify the definitions in the applicable act.

## Step 2: identify sectoral legislation BEFORE the general framework

GPSR is the general safety net. Where sectoral (harmonised) legislation exists, it sets the specific requirements. Categories that trigger special rules (illustrative, not exhaustive):
- electronics and appliances: low voltage, EMC, RoHS, WEEE, batteries;
- wireless devices: radio equipment (including cybersecurity requirements for some categories; check the delegated acts);
- toys and children's articles (check the new toy safety regulation and its application date);
- cosmetics: responsible person, product information file, CPNP portal notification, labelling;
- food and supplements: labelling, national supplement notifications, nutrition and health claims, distance selling;
- textiles and footwear: labelling;
- personal protective equipment;
- machinery;
- chemicals, biocides, detergents: REACH, CLP;
- medical devices (and products claiming medical effects);
- construction products;
- energy-related products: ecodesign, energy labelling;
- food contact materials;
- vehicles, parts, tyres.

If the product does not fit clearly: mark ORANGE and escalate to a product compliance specialist.

## Step 3: GPSR (general framework)

Check:
- the obligations of each economic operator (manufacturer, importer, distributor);
- the **responsible economic operator established in the EU** for products covered by market surveillance rules (see also Regulation 2019/1020);
- **mandatory information in the online offer** (distance sales): manufacturer, EU responsible person, product identification, warnings and safety information; verify the exact list;
- traceability, risk analysis, technical documentation;
- marketplace obligations (single point of contact, registration in Safety Gate, removal of dangerous offers, cooperation);
- recalls: notifying authorities, informing consumers, remedies;
- accident reporting.

## Step 4: imports (dropshipping, non-EU products)

- Who is the importer in customs documents and within the meaning of GPSR? The two roles may differ.
- EORI number for whoever lodges customs declarations.
- Tariff classification (TARIC code), customs duties.
- Import VAT and IOSS for low-value consignments (see domain-tax.md).
- Restrictions, bans, licences, international sanctions.
- Conformity documents from the supplier (declaration of conformity, technical documentation, test reports). Without them you cannot substantiate conformity.

## Step 5: environment and extended producer responsibility (EPR)

Check obligations for:
- packaging (including transport packaging in online commerce); the EU packaging regulation applies in stages, and Romanian legislation has its own reporting and contribution obligations;
- WEEE (electrical equipment);
- batteries;
- textiles (EPR regimes in development; verify);
- marketplace obligations regarding sellers' EPR (verify).

Distance sales to other Member States may create EPR obligations in the destination state (registration, authorised representative). Verify for each targeted state.

## Common pitfalls
- "the supplier has CE" without documents: the marking without declaration and documentation does not prove conformity;
- a dropshipper believing it is not the importer although no other EU operator exists in the actual chain;
- online offers without the safety information required by GPSR;
- applying only GPSR to sector-regulated products;
- ignoring EPR for parcel packaging;
- products labelled "medical", "therapeutic", "antibacterial" without being authorized as such.

## Specific escalation
- possibly dangerous product or consumer incident: product compliance specialist + lawyer, immediately;
- uncertain classification (e.g. supplements vs. medicines, cosmetics vs. medical devices): regulatory specialist in that field;
- imports at volume: customs advisor + tax advisor.
