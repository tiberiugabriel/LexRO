# Domain: intellectual property, competition, advertising

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section H. All verified live.

## 1. Intellectual property

### What to check
- **rights in site materials**: product photos (own, licensed from the supplier, taken from other sites?), texts, fonts, music, video;
- **AI-generated content**: protection regime, the AI provider's terms, risk of reproducing protected works;
- **trademarks**: use of others' marks in ads, keywords, domain names, descriptions ("compatible with X" vs. suggesting an affiliation); is the user's own mark registered (OSIM, EUIPO)? in which classes?
- **counterfeit products** on marketplaces: notice mechanisms (DSA), liability;
- **software**: who owns the code (employees vs. contractors; check the copyright law rules on programs created by employees and assignment in contractor agreements), open-source licences and their compatibility with the business model (e.g. copyleft licences in distributed products);
- **agencies**: does the client contract provide for assignment or licence? does it cover design, code, content? what remains the agency's (reusable components)?
- **databases**: the sui generis right.

### Pitfalls
- product photos copied from the manufacturer's or competitors' sites without a licence;
- "I bought the theme or plugin", so I can do anything with it (check the licence);
- contracts with freelancers without an assignment clause;
- assuming AI output is free of rights.

## 2. Competition

Relevant especially for marketplaces, distribution networks and supplier relationships.

### What to check
- **resale price maintenance** (the supplier imposes a minimum resale price): hardcore restriction;
- **restrictions on online sales** imposed on distributors;
- **parity clauses** (MFN): the platform requires sellers not to sell cheaper elsewhere; the regime differs for wide and narrow parity (check the Vertical Block Exemption Regulation and guidelines);
- **exclusivity**;
- **self-preferencing**: the platform favours its own products in ranking (competition + P2B + DMA for gatekeepers);
- **exchange of sensitive information** between competitors (including via the platform or pricing algorithms);
- **pricing algorithms** that may facilitate collusion.

## 3. Advertising and claims

### What to check
- **misleading advertising** and **comparative advertising** (lawfulness conditions);
- **identifying commercial content**: sponsored posts, influencers, affiliate marketing; clear disclosure of paid collaborations; also check ANPC or Commission guidance on influencers;
- **environmental claims** (Directive 2024/825 and its transposition): prohibition of unsubstantiated generic claims, unverified sustainability labels, climate-neutrality claims based on offsetting (check the exact list and application date);
- **nutrition and health claims** (food, supplements): only authorised claims, under the set conditions;
- **medical claims** for products that are not medicines or medical devices;
- **cosmetics**: common criteria for cosmetic claims;
- **discounts and prices** (see domain-consumer.md);
- **advertising to minors**;
- **targeting** (see domain-cookies-marketing.md).

### Pitfalls
- influencers not disclosing the collaboration;
- "the best", "no. 1" without evidence;
- "100% natural", "eco", "sustainable" without substantiation;
- supplements promising treatment.

## Specific escalation
- IP infringement notice or counterfeiting allegation: IP lawyer;
- exclusivity or parity clauses in high-volume contracts: competition lawyer;
- campaign with health or environmental claims: regulatory specialist + lawyer.
