# Domain: artificial intelligence (AI Act) and data (Data Act)

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section G. All verified live.

## AI Act

### Phased application: always check the date
The AI Act applies in stages (prohibited practices, then general-purpose models, then high-risk systems). According to the compiler, there were EU-level proposals to postpone or simplify some obligations. **Check what applies on the analysis date** before any conclusion.

### Step 1: the role
- **provider** (develops an AI system or places it on the market under its own name);
- **deployer** (uses an AI system in a professional activity);
- importer, distributor.

Most online businesses using AI APIs are deployers; some become providers if they place on the market, under their own name, a system built on a third-party model. Check the definitions.

### Step 2: the risk category
- **prohibited practices** (manipulation exploiting vulnerabilities, certain forms of biometric categorisation, emotion recognition in certain contexts, etc.): check the list;
- **high risk** (the regulation's annexes: e.g. recruitment and worker management, creditworthiness assessment of natural persons; check the list and exceptions);
- **transparency obligations** (check Art. 50 or the corresponding article in the current version):
  - people must be informed they are interacting with an AI system (chatbot), unless obvious;
  - artificially generated or manipulated content (images, audio, video, text under certain conditions) must be marked;
  - deepfakes must be disclosed;
- **AI literacy**: a general obligation for providers and deployers (check the application date and content).

### Typical e-commerce uses and what they trigger
| Use | Check |
|---|---|
| customer chatbot | AI transparency; GDPR (what data reaches the provider) |
| personalized recommendations | GDPR (profiling); DSA (recommender transparency, for platforms) |
| dynamic or personalized pricing | consumer law (information on personalized prices); GDPR (automated decisions) |
| fraud detection, automatic order rejection | GDPR Art. 22; possibly high risk in some contexts (verify) |
| BNPL or credit scoring | high risk (creditworthiness); consumer credit |
| generated descriptions or images | transparency for generated content; intellectual property; commercial practices (misleading images) |
| generated or summarised reviews | commercial practices (fake reviews); transparency |
| AI recruitment | high risk |
| content moderation | DSA (statements of reasons); GDPR |

### Step 3: the AI provider
- is submitted data used for training? (contract and settings)
- where is it processed? (transfer outside the EEA)
- DPA with the provider;
- the general-purpose model provider's documentation (their own obligations).

## Data Act

### Applies if
- you manufacture or sell **connected products** (IoT) or provide **related services** that generate data: obligations on data access and sharing with the user and with third parties designated by them; design requirements;
- you provide **data processing services** (cloud, SaaS under certain conditions): obligations on provider switching, portability, phasing out exit charges; check the classification;
- you have B2B data contracts: unfair contractual terms imposed unilaterally.

For an ordinary online shop, the Data Act is usually marginal. For IoT or cloud it can become central. Check the (phased) application dates.

## Common pitfalls
- a chatbot presented as a human;
- AI-generated product images that do not match the real product;
- sending customer data to an AI model without GDPR analysis;
- assuming the AI Act only concerns companies developing models;
- quoting outdated or unverified application dates.

## Specific escalation
- possibly high-risk system or possibly prohibited practice: specialized lawyer (RED);
- automated decisions affecting people: DPO/lawyer;
- connected products and the Data Act: lawyer.
